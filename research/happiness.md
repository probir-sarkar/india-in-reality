# World Happiness Report — India

## What it measures

The World Happiness Report (WHR) ranks countries by how their residents evaluate their own lives, not by external indicators. The core measure is the Cantril Ladder: each respondent imagines a ladder from 0 (worst possible life) to 10 (best possible life) and places themselves on it. It is a self-reported, subjective measure — a deliberate complement to GDP-style metrics of national progress. The report has been published annually since 2012, founded under the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network (SDSN) and, since 2024/25, produced with the Wellbeing Research Centre at the University of Oxford.

## How it is built

- **Publisher:** Wellbeing Research Centre, University of Oxford, in collaboration with Gallup, the UN Sustainable Development Solutions Network and an independent editorial board (whr2025; tnie2025).
- **Underlying data:** Gallup World Poll surveys, collected since 2005. Roughly 1,000 people per country per year are contacted by phone or face-to-face; in general more than 100,000 people in ~140 countries and territories participate each year (whrfaq2025).
- **Scale mechanics:** the national score is the population-weighted average of individual Cantril Ladder answers (0–10). To reduce sampling error, rankings pool **three years** of responses — the 2025 rankings use 2022–2024 data (whrfaq2025).
- **Explanatory variables:** GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom to make life choices, generosity, and perceptions of corruption. These are regression correlates of the ladder score, not components of it (whrfaq2025; whf2026).
- **Field size:** the number of ranked countries has fluctuated between roughly 137 and 158 across editions; 147 in the 2025 edition (whf2026; tnie2025).

## Credibility

**Strengths**
- One simple, transparent question, translatable without mentioning "happiness", asked consistently since 2005 (whrfaq2025).
- Pooling three years (~3,000 responses/country) reduces random sampling error and keeps countries ranked in years when no survey ran (whrfaq2025).
- The report's authors generally present the six variables as correlates rather than causes of happiness (whf2026).

**Criticisms**
- **Sample size:** ~1,000–3,000 respondents per country per year; India's 2025 sample was reported as ~3,086 people for 1.4 billion — too small to support state-, caste- or income-level breakdowns for a country of India's diversity (moneycontrol2025; whf2026).
- **Cultural response bias:** the ladder framing primes respondents toward wealth/status comparisons, and norms of modesty and middle-scale clustering in South/East Asian respondents can systematically depress reported life evaluations relative to more expressive cultures (whf2026).
- **Cognitive vs felt happiness:** the Cantril Ladder measures a reflective life evaluation, not day-to-day happiness; a society with strong relational bonds can score modestly if aspirations are high — a pattern frequently described for urban India (whf2026).
- **Single-item measurement and translation:** one survey item across dozens of languages has lower construct validity than multi-item scales, a particular concern in multilingual India (whf2026).
- **Moving denominator:** country counts swung between ~137 and ~158 across editions, so raw year-on-year rank jumps partly reflect the changing field, not real change (whf2026).
- **Opacity:** a large "Dystopia" residual, on the order of a fifth of the gap between an average country and the Dystopia baseline, is rarely explained in press coverage (whf2026).
- **India-specific:** a peer-reviewed study argues WHR indicators do not adequately align with Indian realities and questions their suitability for ranking India's happiness (arora2026). Indian happiness researcher Prof. Rajesh Pillania: "Happiness is self-reported, making it prone to subjectivity and bias… our actual happiness levels might be higher than what the report shows" (tribune2025).
- India *is* surveyed annually by Gallup, so there is no missing-year data gap for India in recent editions (whrfaq2025).

## Politics

- India's low rank repeatedly collides with the government's "ease of living" narrative; the New Indian Express framed the 2023 rank against government claims of improvement, and RPG chairman Harsh Goenka tweeted he was "saddened" by India's 2023 performance (tnie2023).
- Spiritual leader Sri Sri Ravi Shankar (22 March 2025): India's ranking "deserves to be much higher… I disagree with India being placed so low, even below conflict zones" (theprint2025).
- **Union Minister Nitin Gadkari**, at the AIIMS Nagpur convocation on 29 March 2025, candidly cited India's 118th rank against Pakistan's 109th and argued that better health outcomes would lift India's standing (loksatta2025). This is the closest thing to an official statement found — no formal government or PIB rebuttal of the WHR was located.
- The India Policy Foundation (a think tank inspired by the RSS) published "In Defence of Inclusion of Happiness in Public Policy for India", arguing the ~3,000-person India sample generalises poorly for 1.4 billion people and urging India to build its own self-reported happiness measurement (moneycontrol2025).
- Widespread netizen backlash followed the 2025 release, with commenters questioning methodology (tnie2025b).

## Real stories

- **Nagpur, 29 March 2025:** at the AIIMS Nagpur convocation, Union Road Transport Minister Nitin Gadkari told graduates that India stands 118th on the world happiness index, behind Pakistan at 109th, and said health is the most important factor that could improve India's rank (loksatta2025).
- **Eating alone, March 2025:** the WHR 2025 records India at 132nd of 147 in meal sharing, averaging about 4 shared meals per week — read by Indian coverage as a marker of urbanisation and increasingly solitary dining among younger Indians, even as India ranks 10th globally for volunteering (tnie2025).

## Gaps

- Exact 2021, 2022 and 2024 India scores (vs ranks) were not captured from the sources seen; ranks only.
- Per-country 2025 ladder scores for China, Nepal, Pakistan, Sri Lanka and Bangladesh were not in the extracted sources; ranks only (from the WHR 2025 PDF itself).
- The Hindu BusinessLine article mislabels report years against ranks in places ("139th in 2020"); this file uses the report-edition year convention consistent with Scroll and the New Indian Express (scroll2022; tnie2025).
- The World Happiness Foundation critical review (whf2026) is published on a foundation portal with a document number rather than a named author; its points are used as secondary analysis, not as a primary source for any number.
- No formal Indian government or PIB statement on the WHR was found; Gadkari's speech is a minister's public remark, not a policy response.

# Human Development Index (HDI) — India

## What it measures

The Human Development Index is a single 0–1 score summarising three things people need for a decent life: a long and healthy life, knowledge (schooling), and a decent standard of living (income). It is published by the United Nations Development Programme (UNDP) in the annual Human Development Report (HDR), which has run since 1990. The HDI was designed as an alternative to ranking countries by GDP alone — a country can be rich and still score poorly on health and education, and vice versa.

## How it is built

- **Publisher:** UNDP Human Development Report Office; the 2025 HDR, "A Matter of Choice: People and Possibilities in the Age of AI", was released 6 May 2025 and ranks 193 countries (hdr2025; undp2025).
- **Inputs (geometric mean of three normalised indices):**
  - Life expectancy at birth (health)
  - Expected years of schooling + mean years of schooling (education)
  - Gross National Income per capita, PPP-adjusted (standard of living)
- **India, HDR 2025 (2023 data):** life expectancy 72.0 years, expected schooling 13.0 years, mean schooling 6.9 years, GNI per capita $9,047 (2021 PPP) — HDI 0.685, rank 130 of 193, "medium human development", just below the high-development threshold of 0.700 (hdrannex2025; undp2025).
- **Inequality-adjusted HDI (IHDI):** discounts each dimension by how unequally it is distributed; the percentage gap between HDI and IHDI is the "loss due to inequality" (deccanherald2025).
- Data lag: the 2025 report carries 2023 indicator values, so headline figures are roughly two years behind the release year (hdr2025).

## Credibility

**Strengths**
- Three decades of a consistent, comparable methodology across 193 countries; one of the few series long enough to show pre/post-1990 development trajectories (nag2023; hdr2025).
- Shifts the yardstick from income to capabilities: India's HDI rank sits well above what its GNI rank alone would imply (GNI-rank-minus-HDI-rank of −7 in 2023) (hdrannex2025).
- UNDP's companion IHDI explicitly corrects the headline for distribution, partly answering the "average achievements" criticism (deccanherald2025).

**Criticisms**
- **Inequality loss:** inequality knocks India's HDI down by 30.7% — from 0.685 to an IHDI of 0.475 — described in Indian coverage as one of the highest losses in the region (deccanherald2025). On inequality-adjusted terms India is a substantially poorer performer than its headline rank suggests.
- **Data lag and possible underestimation:** the 2025 report uses 2023 data; Indian researchers computing a state-level HDI with the UNDP method argue UNDP underestimates India's mean and expected years of schooling and life expectancy by one to two years — on their numbers India's HDI would exceed Bangladesh's (dte2024; nipfp2026).
- **Composite averages hide distribution:** the subnational picture runs from states matching Eastern Europe to states resembling Congo, Kenya, Ghana and Namibia, which the national average erases (hindu2023).
- **Quality vs quantity:** UNDP itself flags that after near-universal enrolment gains, "quality and learning outcomes remain areas for continued focus" — years of schooling say nothing about learning (undp2025).
- **Gender gaps persist:** the report notes female labour-force participation and political representation lag, even as health and education inequality have improved (deccanherald2025).

## Politics

- No government rejection exists; the HDR is welcomed. UNDP India's release (6 May 2025) congratulated India — "rising from rank 133 in 2022 to 130 in 2023" (Angela Lusigi, UNDP India Resident Representative) — and its success story is built on government programmes: National Rural Health Mission, Ayushman Bharat, Janani Suraksha Yojana, Poshan Abhiyaan, the Right to Education Act, Samagra Shiksha, NEP 2020, MGNREGA and Jan Dhan Yojana (undp2025; deccanherald2025).
- Indian officialdom's main counter-framing comes through state-linked research rather than rebuttal: State Bank of India's subnational HDI work argued south and west states lead while eastern states lag, and a SBI-affiliated analysis claimed the UNDP understates India's schooling and life expectancy (tnie2019; dte2024).
- No PIB press release responding to the 2025 HDR was located (see Gaps).

## Real stories

- **Kerala vs the north (2023, Nature Portfolio journal):** recomputed on the UNDP method, Kerala's state HDI was 0.782 in 2019 — first among Indian states and comparable to countries in the "high human development" band — while Bihar's was 0.574, last of 36 states and UTs, barely above India's own 1990s level. The Kerala–Bihar gap (~0.21) is roughly the distance between India's national HDI today and its level three decades ago (nag2023).
- **Two countries within one (2023, The Hindu):** in the subnational HDI, Delhi and Goa score above 0.799 — equivalent to Eastern European countries — while the bottom five (Bihar, Uttar Pradesh, Madhya Pradesh, Jharkhand, Assam) score at levels resembling Congo, Kenya, Ghana and Namibia; Gujarat and Haryana, despite among the highest GSDP per capita, fail to convert income into human development (hindu2023). The NIPFP update for 2022-23 finds the picture shifting: Kerala slipped from 1st to 5th, Chhattisgarh fell to last, while Odisha, Bihar and Uttar Pradesh improved their consumption-driven rankings (nipfp2026).

## Gaps

- The 30.7% inequality loss and IHDI 0.475 are from Deccan Herald's report on the HDR 2025 (deccanherald2025) plus multiple secondary write-ups; the UNDP Statistical Annex Table 3 (IHDI) itself was not directly fetched, so the exact IHDI value should be re-verified against the annex before publication.
- The official UNDP 1990–2019 trend series was not directly retrieved; the trend table uses Nag & Pradhan's (2023) peer-reviewed recomputation with the UNDP method, which may differ from UNDP's official trend values in early years.
- UNDP's own figures moved between editions: the HDR 2023/24 cycle reported India 2022 at 0.644 / rank 134, while the HDR 2025 lists 2022 at 0.676 / rank 133 (recalculated indicators). Both are UNDP numbers; the file uses the HDR 2025 revision.
- The Down To Earth subnational-HDI piece (dte2024) lacks a captured publication date; it discusses 2022-23 estimates and cites UNDP's then-latest value (0.644), implying 2024 publication — verify exact date.
- No PIB statement or formal government reaction to HDR 2025 was found; coverage of the government's position relies on the UNDP India release, which incorporates government programme framing.

# Environment — Yale Environmental Performance Index (EPI)

## What it measures

The Environmental Performance Index (EPI) ranks countries on how close they are to
established environmental policy targets. It combines two broad things: the state of the
environment people live in (air quality, water and sanitation, heavy metals) and the health
of ecosystems (biodiversity, forests, fisheries, agriculture), plus, in recent editions, a
separate climate-change objective tracking emissions trends and projected emissions. Scores
run 0–100, where 100 means meeting the target. India has been at or near the bottom of the
ranking in every edition since 2022 — last of 180 in 2022, 176th of 180 in 2024, and
second-last of 177 in 2026 (yale2022, yale2024b, yale2026).

## How it is built

- Published every two years by the Yale Center for Environmental Law & Policy (Yale University) and the Center for International Earth Science Information Network (CIESIN), Columbia University (yale2024b, theprint2024).
- The 2024 edition scored 180 countries on 58 indicators grouped under three objectives — ecosystem vitality, environmental health, and climate change (theprint2024).
- The 2026 edition ranks 177 countries on 47 indicators across 12 issue categories under the same three policy objectives (yale2026).
- Each indicator is scored 0–100 against a target (e.g., WHO air-quality guidelines), then weighted up a hierarchy: indicator → issue category → policy objective → overall EPI (yale2024b).
- Inputs include satellite-derived data (e.g., Copernicus atmospheric composition for PM2.5 and air quality) and the Global Burden of Disease study for health outcomes such as pollution-related DALYs (pib2022, downtoearth2022).
- A companion ranking, the Climate Change Performance Index (CCPI) published by Germanwatch, NewClimate Institute and Climate Action Network, tracks climate mitigation in 63 countries + the EU using GHG emissions (40%), renewable energy (20%), energy use (20%) and expert-assessed climate policy (20%) (germanwatch2025).

## Credibility

Strengths:
- Long-running, academically led, fully documented methodology with downloadable per-country data files, going back to 2002 (theprint2024, yale2022).
- Ranks on measured current conditions, not promises: "EPI has always based our rankings on country performance today; not on what a country promises to accomplish years or decades from now" — Martin Wolf, EPI principal investigator (downtoearth2022).
- The air-quality/health signal is grounded in the Global Burden of Disease: millions of premature deaths in India are attributed to poor ambient air quality every year (downtoearth2022).

Criticisms:
- The Indian government's core objection: the "projected 2050 GHG emissions" indicator extrapolates the last 10 years' emissions trend instead of modelling policy, renewables and carbon sinks, and carries heavy weight (~14%) while per-capita-emissions equity indicators carry ~1% (pib2022, pib2022b).
- India-specific data gap the ministry flagged: Copernicus satellite air-pollution data have higher uncertainty where ground monitoring networks are sparse, which "reduces the chance of accurate assessment of Air Quality" in India (pib2022).
- The index measures extent of protected areas, not their management quality, and omits water quality, water-use efficiency and waste-generation-per-capita indicators (pib2022).
- Yale's response: the weights are a balanced scorecard, not adjusted to punish countries; rejecting a data-driven tool outright is "unproductive and perilous" (downtoearth2022).
- Even India-friendly rankings show slippage: in the CCPI — which credits low per-capita emissions — India fell 13 places to 23rd in 2026, last among ranked countries on emissions trends, with no coal exit timeline (germanwatch2025, ccpi2026).
- Genuine Indian data gaps affecting all environmental assessment: the 2021 Census was not conducted, and the EPI's health inputs rely on global modelled datasets (GBD, Copernicus) rather than dense Indian monitoring (pib2022, indiaforum).

## Politics

- 8 June 2022: MoEFCC formally rejected EPI 2022 (India ranked 180/180) in a PIB release: the index "has many indicators based on unfounded assumptions. Some of these indicators used for assessing performance are extrapolated and based on surmises and unscientific methods," and "the Ministry of Environment does not accept its analysis and conclusions" (pib2022).
- Same statement argued equity is under-weighted: "The principle of equity is given very low weightage in the form of the indicators like GHG emission per capita... The CBDR-RC principle is also barely reflected," and noted India had already achieved 40% non-fossil installed capacity nine years ahead of its 2030 pledge (pib2022).
- July 2022: a second MoEFCC release, "Environmental Performance Index 2022 does not consider several important indicators of sustainable consumption and production," repeated that the metrics are "unscientific and subjective" and detailed the 14%-vs-1% weighting complaint (pib2022b).
- Yale's rebuttal: lead scientist Martin Wolf said the government "decided to reject a useful data-driven tool instead of leveraging its findings," and denied weights were manipulated against India (downtoearth2022).
- No equivalent government rejection of the CCPI — official rhetoric instead cites India's decade-high CCPI positions (top-10 for six years till 2024) and the 50% non-fossil milestone; the 2026 fall to 23rd went unremarked by government releases (economictimes2022, pib2025, toi2025).
- The 40%-non-fossil claim in the PIB rebuttal later hardened into fact: PIB announced 50% achieved on 14 July 2025, five years ahead of the NDC deadline, and Reuters confirmed the milestone (pib2025, reuters2025).

## Real stories

- **The Yamuna's toxic foam, Delhi, November 2024.** During Chhath Puja, thousands of devotees waded into the Yamuna in Delhi to pray despite a Delhi High Court order ("Please understand you will fall sick") restricting ritual bathing; the river ran with thick white foam from detergents and phosphates. A February 2024 parliamentary report had called the Yamuna "more of a toxic waterway than a river," noting heavy metals "from arsenic to zinc"; government statistics attribute ~80% of its pollution load to raw sewage, and that same week Delhi's PM2.5 exceeded 50 times the WHO daily maximum (france24_2024). This is the lived reality behind India's EPI bottom-tier air-quality and water scores.
- **Air pollution's death toll.** The EPI's health inputs (Global Burden of Disease) attribute more than 1.6 million deaths a year in India — about 18% of all deaths — to air pollution, the single biggest driver of India's last-place 2022 EPI rank (mongabay2022, downtoearth2022).

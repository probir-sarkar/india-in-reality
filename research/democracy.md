# Democracy — V-Dem, Freedom House and EIU Democracy Index (India)

Three different well-known assessments call India's democracy into question in different ways: the V-Dem Institute (University of Gothenburg) classifies India an "electoral autocracy"; Freedom House rates India "partly free"; the EIU Democracy Index rates India a "flawed democracy". This file covers all three.

## What it measures

- **V-Dem** produces the largest global dataset on democracy — over 600 attributes, 202 countries, 1789–2024, over 31 million data points — and classifies each country by regime type: liberal democracy, electoral democracy, electoral autocracy or closed autocracy. India has been classified an "electoral autocracy" since the March 2021 Democracy Report, with V-Dem dating the start of India's autocratisation episode to around 2017 (confirmed by 2019) (vdem2021; thewire2025).
- **Freedom House** (Washington, DC) rates countries Free / Partly Free / Not Free on a 0–100 score combining political rights and civil liberties. India was downgraded from Free to Partly Free in the Freedom in the World 2021 report (diplomat2021; freedomhouse2021).
- **EIU Democracy Index** (Economist Intelligence Unit) scores 167 countries 0–10 on 60 indicators in five categories — electoral process and pluralism, functioning of government, political participation, political culture, civil liberties — and classifies full democracy (8–10), flawed democracy (6–8), hybrid regime (4–6), authoritarian (0–4). India has been a "flawed democracy" throughout the recent series (eiu-di2022; theprint2023).

## How it is built

**V-Dem**
- Country experts code countries on hundreds of fine-grained indicators each year; V-Dem aggregates them into indices (electoral democracy, liberal democracy, egalitarian, participatory, deliberative components) using measurement models that account for expert uncertainty (thewire2025).
- Regime classification thresholds sit on the electoral-democracy index and related measures; reports are published each March by the V-Dem Institute, University of Gothenburg (vdem2021; vdem2025).

**Freedom House**
- Annual Freedom in the World report scores 195 countries on 25 indicators across political rights (0–4 subscores per question) and civil liberties, assessed by analysts and an expert panel; the total maps to the three status bands. Indian Kashmir is assessed separately (fh2025; scroll2023).

**EIU**
- 60 expert-assessed indicators (mostly 0/1 or 0/0.5/1) grouped into the five categories, combined into a 0–10 index; expert assessments are informed by public-opinion surveys (e.g., World Values Survey) and electoral data. The EIU itself cautions these are "not thick enough" measures of substantive democracy (theprint2023; eiu-di2022).

## Credibility

Strengths:
- V-Dem's scale (600+ attributes, 31 million data points, uncertainty estimates from multiple experts per country) is unmatched among democracy measures (thewire2025).
- Freedom House and EIU publish long, consistent annual series with transparent status bands, and all three show broadly consistent direction for India since 2014 (fh2025; eiu-di2024; thewire2025).

Criticisms:
- The Government of India's own Economic Advisory Council (EAC-PM working paper by Sanjeev Sanyal and Akanksha Arora) attacked all three indices: they rest on "the opinions of a tiny group of unknown experts", use questions that are subjective and "impossible to answer objectively", omit relevant questions, and are not appropriate to compare across countries; it noted FH and V-Dem place India at the same level as the 1975–77 Emergency (pib2023).
- Scholars have pushed back on V-Dem's India classification specifically: Siddharth Singh (Open Magazine) argues the project is "not an intellectual exercise but an ideological one", pointing to results such as El Salvador, Honduras and Sierra Leone ranking above India on electoral democracy (open2024); Salvatore Babones (2026, in a SAGE journal) argues "thick" democracy indices like V-Dem are structurally biased against the Global South (babones2026).
- V-Dem country scores depend on small panels of anonymous country experts whose selection criteria are not public; small changes in expert pools can move scores and ranks (open2024; pib2023).
- Freedom House is a US-based advocacy organisation; critics question the transferability of its rubric, and the Indian government has called its political judgements "inaccurate and distorted" (scroll2023; pib2023).
- The EIU acknowledges its own measures "are not thick enough… they do not encompass sufficiently, or, in some cases, at all, the features that determine how substantive democracy is" (theprint2023).
- India-specific data gaps: Freedom House's India score excludes Indian Kashmir (assessed separately) (fh2025); EIU's Democracy Index 2025 India rank/score could not be verified from a primary source for this file (see Gaps).

## Politics

- March 2021: after the V-Dem "electoral autocracy" classification and Freedom House's downgrade, External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar called the indices' authors "self-appointed custodians of the world" and their exercise "hypocrisy" ("they invent their rules, their parameters, they pass their judgments and then make out as though this is some kind of global exercise") (diplomat2021; scroll2023).
- March 2021: the Ministry of External Affairs, in an internal note reported by Hindustan Times, considered commissioning a "global democracy index" and "global press freedom index" by an Indian think tank, and suggested Indian missions feed material to RSF and V-Dem to put "India at the rightful place on the democracy and press-freedom index, in future reports" (diplomat2021; ht2021).
- March 19, 2021: Foreign Policy reported on the government's counter-campaign after the "electoral autocracy" label ("India's 'Electoral Autocracy' Hits Back") (fp2021).
- February 2023: PIB carried the EAC-PM working paper "Why India does poorly on global perception indices", attacking the methodology of the Freedom House, V-Dem and EIU indices and urging Indian think tanks to build rival indices; it also asked the World Bank to ensure "greater transparency and accountability" since these indices feed the World Governance Indicators (pib2023).
- 2026: opposition leaders used V-Dem's data against the government — Congress leader P. Chidambaram flagged India's rank 106/179 and "lowest score since 1975" on the Electoral Democracy Index on X (et2026).

## Real stories

- 146 opposition MPs suspended from Parliament: During the December 2023 winter session, opposition MPs demanding a Home Minister statement on the December 13 Parliament security breach were suspended in waves — 14 on December 14, then 78, 49 and more — taking the total to 146 Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha MPs by December 21, 2023. Major bills, including the Chief Election Commissioner appointment bill and the Telecommunications Bill, were passed while the opposition was largely suspended (indiatoday2023).
- Electoral bonds struck down: On February 15, 2024, the Supreme Court of India declared the electoral bonds political-funding scheme unconstitutional, calling it a setback for the BJP which had raised large sums through the anonymous instrument; the court ended a scheme that had kept corporate donors secret from voters (reuters2024).
- ED cases against the opposition: An Indian Express investigation of court records and agency statements (September 21, 2022) found 121 prominent politicians came under Enforcement Directorate probe after 2014, of whom 115 (95%) were opposition leaders — against 26 politicians probed in the entire UPA decade (2004–14), fueling the "ED is the new CBI" perception (indianexpress2022).

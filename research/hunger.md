# Hunger — India in Reality

## What it measures

The Global Hunger Index (GHI) scores countries on hunger on a 0–100 scale where 0 is best, placing each country in a category from "low" to "extremely alarming" (20.0–34.9 = "serious"). It is deliberately broader than food output: it combines population-level calorie deprivation with child undernutrition and child deaths, on the logic that hunger's worst effects show up in children. India has scored in the "serious" band in every recent edition, behind most of its South Asian neighbours.

## How it is built

- Published annually by Concern Worldwide (Ireland) and Welthungerhilfe (Germany), with the IFHV (Ruhr University Bochum); peer-reviewed (ghi2026, ghi_methodology).
- Four equally weighted indicators: (1) undernourishment — share of population with insufficient caloric intake, from FAO's State of Food Security and Nutrition in the World (SOFI); (2) child stunting — under-fives with low height-for-age; (3) child wasting — under-fives with low weight-for-height, both from the UNICEF–WHO–World Bank Joint Malnutrition Estimates (JME); (4) under-five mortality — UN Inter-agency Group for Child Mortality Estimation (ghi_methodology).
- Score is the one-third-weighted average of the indicators (stunting and wasting share a combined one-third weight). Scores are recalculated each year; comparisons over time should use the reference years given in each report (ghi_methodology).
- Countries with a score under 5 are collectively ranked (China is one of these); 122 countries had sufficient data in 2026 (ghi2026).

## Credibility

Strengths:
- Inputs come from established UN inter-agency processes — FAO SOFI for undernourishment, UNICEF–WHO–World Bank JME for stunting/wasting, UN IGME for child mortality — not from the publishers' own polling (ghi_methodology).
- It captures both acute (wasting) and chronic (stunting) undernutrition plus a mortality outcome, and is recalculated transparently with published indicator tables (ghi_methodology, ghi2026pdf).
- India's own NFHS-5 survey corroborates the scale of child undernutrition: stunting 35.5%, wasting 19.3%, severe wasting 7.7% (nfhs5).
- GHI is explicit about data limitations, marking provisional values and not ranking countries with insufficient data (ghi2026, ghi2026pdf).

Criticisms and India-specific data gaps:
- India's child-wasting number is the core dispute: GHI 2023 used 18.7% from the UN JME, while the government's Poshan Tracker — real-time measurements of 7+ crore under-fives uploaded by Anganwadi workers — consistently showed wasting below 7.2% in 2023 (pib_ghi2023). NFHS-5's own survey figure (19.3% wasted) sits close to the JME number, so the government's case rests on administrative data vs survey data (nfhs5, pib_ghi2023).
- The undernourishment indicator is FAO-modelled; the government objects that India's PoU (16.3% in 2022) rests on the Food Insecurity Experience Scale administered through the Gallup World Poll to only about 3,000 Indian respondents (pib_ghi2022).
- Three of four indicators concern children under five, which the government argues cannot represent the whole population's hunger (pib_ghi2022, pib_ghi2023).
- GHI's own methodology note says historical scores are recalculated annually and only reference-year comparisons (2000, 2006, 2012, 2018 in the 2026 report) are valid (ghi_methodology).
- India-specific gap: GHI child indicators rely on JME, which draws on Indian surveys of varying vintage; India has not had a fresh national nutrition survey since NFHS-5 (2019–21), and the 2026 GHI marks India's wasting value as provisional (ghi2026pdf).

## Politics

- **15 October 2022** — After GHI 2022 ranked India 107th of 121, the Women and Child Development Ministry issued a PIB statement titled "The index is an erroneous measure of hunger and suffers from serious methodological issues", calling it "a consistent effort… to taint India's image as a Nation that does not fulfill the food security and nutritional requirements of its population", attacking the Gallup-based PoU ("an opinion poll… sample size of 3000") and saying the publishers "evidently… have not done their due diligence" (pib_ghi2022).
- **12 October 2023** — After GHI 2023 ranked India 111th of 125 with the world's highest child-wasting rate (18.7%), PIB said the index "continues to be… erroneous… and shows a malafide intent", contrasting it with Poshan Tracker data: 72.4 crore (7.24 crore) children measured, wasting "consistently below 7.2%, month-on-month" (pib_ghi2023).
- **October 2024** — GHI 2024 (rank 105 of 127, score 27.3) drew a renewed Ministry of Women and Child Development objection to using JME rather than India's own programme data; The Hindu's Explained noted the ministry's concern that "prevalence rates across the world come from independent surveys" was being answered by Poshan Tracker (hindu2024).
- No PIB statement specifically addressing the GHI 2026 edition had been verified as of writing (see Gaps).

## Real stories

- **Santoshi Kumari, 11, Karimati village, Simdega, Jharkhand — died 28 September 2017.** Scroll.in reported that Santoshi died after going days with barely any food during the Durga Puja holidays, when school mid-day meals stopped; her family's ration card had been cancelled because it was not linked to Aadhaar, cutting off their National Food Security Act rations. Reuters (18 October 2017) covered the death as sparking a national debate on tying welfare to identity cards; officials disputed the cause (the deputy commissioner said malaria), and a year later Scroll.in documented rising "Aadhaar tragedies" in Jharkhand's ration system (scroll2017, reuters2017, scroll2018, ht2017).
- **Poshan Tracker as counter-evidence.** The government's own platform — 13.96 lakh Anganwadi centres registered, 10.3 crore beneficiaries, WHO-recognised for routine nutrition data — became the state's rebuttal to the GHI: 7.24 crore children under five measured, with wasting below 7.2% every month from April to September 2023 (pib_ghi2023).
